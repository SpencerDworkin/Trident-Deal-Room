What we do

The opening bid is the debt. Everything Trident does follows from that.

A sheriff’s sale is the one place in residential property where the price is set by a number on a court record rather than by a seller. Trident finds the filings where that number sits far below the house, buys only those, and holds them for the rent.

01

How a sheriff’s sale works

New Jersey is a judicial-foreclosure state. A lender that wants to take a house must sue, obtain a final judgment of foreclosure for what it is owed — principal, interest, fees and costs — and have the court direct the county sheriff to sell the property at public auction. The sale is scheduled weeks out and listed on the county’s calendar with the case number, the address, the plaintiff and the judgment amount.

At the auction the lender opens the bidding at its judgment, or at an “upset” figure it announces, and anyone may bid above it. The winner pays twenty percent in certified funds at the gavel and the balance within thirty days. The former owner has ten days to redeem. Then the sheriff’s deed is delivered, and the buyer owns what the sale conveyed — and what it did not extinguish.

None of this is hidden. The judgment is on the docket, the sale is on the calendar, the plaintiff is named. A buyer who reads the record knows the price floor before walking in.

02

Why the debt, not the market

The judgment is the borrower’s debt, not the house’s worth. A house bought in 2004 and foreclosed in 2026 may carry a judgment of $120,000 against a county median of $540,000; the lender bids to its $120,000 and is generally content to be made whole. A house bought in 2022 with three percent down carries a judgment at or above what it would sell for; the lender bids to that, and there is nothing to win.

So the single most useful number about a filing is recorded debt as a fraction of county median value. Trident computes it for every scheduled sale in its counties and sorts the calendar by it. Below 0.4 is a deep discount; 0.4 to 0.6 a discount; 0.6 to 0.8 possible. From 0.8 up there is no margin for a title, occupancy or make-ready surprise, and the screen refuses the file.

This is not a claim that the market is wrong. It is an observation that the auction’s reserve price is set by a different variable than the asset’s value, and that the two can be compared in advance from public records. How the current book sorts →

03

From the calendar to the deed

Every property runs the same sequence. The dates hang off the sale date, so when a sale is adjourned the whole schedule moves. Nine of the ten checklist items block a bid until done; a file that reaches sale day with one open is not bid on, whatever the discount.

  • Dailyevery pullScreen the calendar.Every scheduled sale is pulled from the county sheriff, matched against the buy box, and scored on debt to median. A changed date or judgment is recorded as an event on the file, so an adjournment is a fact rather than a surprise at the courthouse.
  • T−21Order the sixty-year title search.Senior liens, municipal charges and federal tax liens survive the sheriff’s deed. Whatever the search finds is carried in the basis, not discovered afterwards.
  • T−14Independent valuation.County medians and assessor data are estimates. The exit value is confirmed before a deposit is exposed, and the ratio recomputed.
  • T−10Municipal search, occupancy, first-refusal status.Open violations and unpaid utilities ride with the parcel. Occupancy is established from the outside, because there is no interior access and New Jersey is a good-cause eviction state. And the Community Wealth Preservation Program is checked: a foreclosed owner-occupant may take the property at the opening bid, which ends the file.
  • T−7Read the title, set the lien reserve.What the deed extinguishes is separated from what survives. The surviving total goes into the basis and the ceiling is recomputed. Several files end here.
  • T−3Set the ceiling and the walk-away.From the underwriting, not the room: the bid at which the property still clears a 5% unlevered yield on the county’s Fair Market Rent after surviving liens, make-ready and carry. Written down. Never raised at the auction.
  • T−1Confirm the sale is going forward.Plaintiff’s counsel is called. Adjournments are not re-advertised. Certified funds are staged.
  • SaleBid to the ceiling, and no further.A principal attends. If bidding passes the walk-away, we stop. Twenty percent of the winning bid at the gavel.
  • T+10Redemption lapses.Until it does, the deposit rides.
  • T+30Balance paid, deed delivered.Missing the window forfeits the deposit, which is why the funds are in hand before the bid.
  • AfterMake-ready, inspection, lease.Possession is resolved according to what the occupancy screen found. The property is brought to the housing authority’s inspection standard and leased to a voucher holder.

04

The hold: Housing Choice Vouchers

Trident does not flip. Each property is leased under the federal Housing Choice Voucher program, in which the local housing authority pays the larger part of the rent directly to the owner each month and the tenant pays the remainder. The rent is anchored to the county’s published Fair Market Rent.

The appeal is not a premium rent. It is the reliability of the payer and the length of the tenancy. A voucher tenant who has found a home that passes inspection tends to stay, and the authority’s share arrives whether or not the tenant has had a good month.

Every county is modelled on its own Fair Market Rent against a 5% unlevered stabilised yield. A county that cannot clear the hurdle at its FMR is excluded from the voucher strategy whatever its discounts; Burlington currently models at 3.98% and is out. Each county’s figures →

05

What survives the sale

A sheriff’s deed conveys the property subject to whatever the judgment did not extinguish. The discount is real only after these are priced. Each is paired with the step that handles it; the disclosures list the risks in full.

  • Surviving liens.Senior mortgages, municipal charges, unpaid utilities and federal tax liens can all survive the deed. — The sixty-year search at T−21 and the lien reserve at T−7; surviving totals go into the basis before the ceiling is set.
  • It was never a first mortgage.Debt at six percent of median is likelier a subordinate lien, a private judgment or an association action, with the senior mortgage surviving. — A $20,000 floor, a flag below 0.12, and the title search as the final word on position.
  • Owner-occupant first refusal.Under New Jersey’s Community Wealth Preservation Program a foreclosed owner-occupant may take the property at the opening bid. — Checked at T−10 before funds are committed.
  • Occupancy and possession.No interior access before the sale; a good-cause eviction state. — An exterior and records-based occupancy screen, with possession costs priced into make-ready before the ceiling is set.
  • Condition unseen.Make-ready is an estimate until the door opens. — A make-ready allowance in every ceiling and a yield hurdle that leaves room for the estimate to be wrong.
  • Adjournment.Sales move, often without re-advertisement. — The daily pull records every date change and moves the schedule; the call to counsel at T−1 catches the rest.
