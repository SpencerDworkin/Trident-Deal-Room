Investors

Trident is raising its first fund.

A private fund that buys at the courthouse, for cash, and holds for income. Raised from family offices in New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Connecticut and Florida. The confidential memorandum sets out every term and controls over anything here; this is the shape of it.

01

How capital moves

Investors commit capital to the fund. The fund buys properties outright at sheriff’s sale, brings each to the housing authority’s inspection standard, leases it to a voucher holder and holds it. Rents pay operating costs and reserves first; what remains is distributed on the terms in the memorandum. The principals invest in the fund alongside investors.

Cash, not debt. A sheriff’s sale takes twenty percent at the gavel and the balance in thirty days, in certified funds. There is no financing contingency and no lender at the table. Buying for cash is not a choice the fund makes; it is the only way to buy at the courthouse. It also means no lender ranks ahead of investors on the properties.

Cash that waits. The screen refuses 37% of the current calendar and the checklist blocks bids on files that are not ready. Some quarters there will be more capital than qualifying sales. The fund holds it rather than bending the screen to spend it.

03

Who we raise from

Family offices in New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania and Connecticut, because an allocator who can drive to a property in an afternoon is an allocator who understands what they own; and Florida, because a good many of the same families are there for part of the year.

The offering is private, to accredited investors and qualified purchasers, with a minimum commitment of $1,000,000. We are not raising from the public, and we would rather have a small number of investors who have walked a property than a large number who have read a deck.

04

Reporting

Trident runs on one working book: every filing in the covered counties, its screen result, and for every property the fund pursues, the file — title findings, lien reserve, occupancy, ceiling, what it paid, what make-ready cost, when it leased and at what rent. Investor reporting is that book, quarterly. There is nothing to reconcile between what you are told and what we look at, because it is the same document. A property that disappoints shows up on the same page, the same quarter.

05

Questions investors ask first

What am I invested in?

An interest in the fund, which owns the properties. Distributions come from the rents those properties generate after costs and reserves, and from any eventual sale. The fund is the owner of record; you own the fund.

Where does my money sit before it is deployed?

In cash, in the fund’s account. Deployment follows the sheriff’s calendar and the screen, not a schedule we set. We would rather hold cash than bid on a file the screen refused.

Are distributions guaranteed?

No. They are paid from the properties’ cash flow on the terms in the memorandum. Trident is newly formed; there is no track record behind any projection, only the method.

Is there debt on the properties?

Not in the base structure. A sheriff’s sale requires certified funds, so the fund buys for cash. If the fund were ever to borrow, the memorandum sets the limits and you would be told.

What happens when a sale is adjourned, or we are outbid?

Nothing is spent. Capital for a property is committed only at the gavel. An adjourned sale moves and the file waits; where bidding passes our ceiling we walk away. Most of the work on a file happens before the sale and costs time, not capital.

What are the main risks?

Surviving liens, occupancy and possession, condition unseen before purchase, the owner-occupant right of first refusal, adjournment, dependence on the voucher program, and a newly formed manager. What we do pairs each with the step that handles it; the disclosures list them in full.

Who can invest, and what is the minimum?

Accredited investors and qualified purchasers, by private placement. The minimum commitment is $1,000,000.

Can I see the properties?

After acquisition, yes — walk any property the fund owns. Before the sale there is no interior access for anyone, including us. Each property also appears in the quarterly book with photographs.

How are you paid?

The manager’s compensation and participation are in the memorandum. The principals also invest in the fund on the same terms as other investors. We would rather explain the economics on a call than reduce them to a line here.
