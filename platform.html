The Sheriff Sale Desk

Distressed real estate is not a data problem or a courage problem. It is a speed problem. Between the day a notice of sale publishes and the day the gavel drops, someone has to value the parcel, scope the work, price the money, and read the title. We built a desk that does all four before the sale date.

Why most bidders lose

The auction rewards preparation, not nerve

Six counties publish foreclosure notices on their own schedules, in their own formats, with no valuation, no condition read, and no financing context. A bidder who works the list by hand can properly diligence maybe four properties a week. There are usually eighty on the calendar.

So most people bid on the two they know and skip the seventy-eight they don't. The desk exists to close that gap: every filing gets the same treatment, every time, before the sale.

What has to happen before the gavel

  • Value it. An opening bid means nothing without an after-repair value built from paired comparable sales.
  • Scope it. Condition drives the number. A cosmetic refresh and a full gut are $40,000 apart on the same footprint.
  • Price the money. Bridge terms, DSCR floors, and the takeout you will actually qualify for decide the ceiling on your bid.
  • Read the title. Lien position, surviving municipal charges, and possession risk are what turn a good buy into a bad year.

The agent network

Sixteen agents, four stages, one file

Each agent owns one narrow job and hands its output to the next. Nothing is a black box: every number on a deal card traces back to the agent that produced it, and every agent's confidence is scored separately so you can see which part of a file is weak.

The pipeline

Notice of sale to recorded deed

A typical file moves through the desk in under 48 hours from publication, which leaves the rest of the redemption window for the only two things that need a human: an interior look and a bid decision.

Publication

The notice hits the county feed. The docket agent normalizes it and opens a file within the hour.

Enrichment

Assessor, permit, tax, and sales history attach to the parcel. Gaps are flagged rather than guessed.

Underwriting

Review

Three exits, modeled on every file

The same house is a different deal depending on your exit

We do not decide your strategy for you. Every property is underwritten against all three, and the deal card tells you which ones actually clear.

Strategy 01

Flip

Buy at the sale, renovate to a retail standard, resell inside six to nine months. The screen is the classic 70% rule with the rehab budget deducted, tested against real selling costs and carry.

  • Maximum allowable offer reported on every file
  • Net profit after commission, closing, and holding carry
  • Return measured on cash actually invested, not on price
Strategy 02

Buy & Hold

Stabilize and keep it. The screen is whether the asset services its own debt with room to spare once vacancy, management, maintenance, and capital reserves are honestly funded.

  • NOI built on effective gross income, not gross rent
  • DSCR tested against lender floors, not aspiration
  • Yield on cost measured against all-in basis
Strategy 03

BRRRR

Buy, rehab, rent, refinance, repeat. The screen is how much of your capital comes back out at the refinance and whether the post-refi payment still clears the coverage test.

  • Cash returned at the refinance and capital left in the deal
  • Post-refinance cash flow and coverage ratio
  • Seasoning and LTV constraints priced in up front
