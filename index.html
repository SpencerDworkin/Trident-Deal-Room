The firm

A sheriff’s sale sets its price from a court record, not a seller.

The lender opens the bidding at its judgment. The judgment is what the borrower owes, not what the house is worth, and the two can be compared in advance, from public records, for every scheduled sale. Trident does that every day across six counties and buys only where they are furthest apart.

How it works

What we do

Screen. Buy. Hold.

01 · Screen

Every filing, the same rules

Each scheduled sale in six counties is pulled daily, matched against a nine-rule buy box and scored on recorded debt against county median value.

How we buy 02 · Buy

For cash, to a written ceiling

Title, valuation, occupancy and liens are worked on a dated checklist before the sale. A principal attends, bids to a ceiling set from the underwriting, and stops there.

What we do 03 · Hold

Voucher tenancy, for income

Each home is brought to the housing authority’s inspection standard and leased to a Housing Choice Voucher holder. The authority pays the larger part of the rent directly, every month.

For investors

Approach

Discipline is the product.

The screen is written down. The buy box is nine rules with numbers in them, run on every filing in every covered county in the same order. Neither principal can override a verdict for a property they like; the rules change for everyone or not at all.

Cash, not debt. A sheriff’s sale takes twenty percent at the gavel and the balance in thirty days, in certified funds. There is no lender at the table and no lender ahead of investors on the properties.

A principal at every sale. The ceiling is set from the underwriting three days out and written down. The person in the room is the person who set the number, and the number does not move.

Cash that waits. Some quarters there will be more capital than qualifying sales. The fund holds it rather than bending the screen to spend it.

Where

Six New Jersey counties today. National in ambition.

Bergen, Burlington, Hunterdon, Mercer, Middlesex and Monmouth are live; Ocean and Somerset are being brought on. Any judicial-foreclosure state with public sale calendars and a working voucher program is a candidate.
