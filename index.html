The strategy

Where the price is set by the debt.

At a sheriff’s sale the foreclosing lender opens the bidding at its judgment — the balance of the loan, interest and costs. Where a borrower has paid the mortgage down, or bought long ago, that figure sits well below what the house would bring on the open market. The gap is the opportunity, and it is visible before the sale: the judgment is public record.

Trident screens every scheduled sale across six New Jersey counties against county median values and bids only where recorded debt sits at a deep discount to value. The number comes off the docket, not from a broker.

01 — SCREEN

Priced on the record

Every filing is scored on the ratio of recorded debt to county median. Below 40% is a deep discount. Above 80% we refuse. Above 100% the lender will bid to its own debt and there is no discount to win.

02 — BID

Bought with a ceiling

A bid ceiling is set before the sale from the yield the property can carry, and it is never raised in the room. Twenty percent in certified funds at the gavel; the balance in thirty days; redemption lapses in ten.

03 — HOLD

Held for yield

Properties are converted to Housing Choice Voucher tenancy — rent paid substantially by the housing authority, on a schedule. Each is underwritten to a 5% unlevered yield on the county’s Fair Market Rent before any lift is assumed.

The discipline

A buy box written as rules, applied to every filing.

Trident does not have a track record to show you yet, and will not pretend to. What it has is a method that runs the same way on every filing, and the current book is the evidence. Every rule in the buy box is a line of code, so the screen cannot be bent for a property we happen to like.

We also know who is filing. One lender accounts for nearly a fifth of the current calendar, at a median debt-to-value of 0.82 — thin. Another’s thirty-one filings sit at 0.58. The plaintiff tells you the discount before you open the file.

What we refuse

The refusals matter as much as the buys. A sheriff’s sale forgives nothing: senior liens, municipal charges and tax liens survive the deed. The screen exists to keep us out of the files where the discount is an illusion.

  • Debt above the county median. The lender bids to its own debt; there is no discount to win.
  • Debt implausibly small for a first mortgage. Below 12% of median it is likelier a subordinate lien or a private judgment — and the senior debt survives the sale.
  • Thin margins. Between 80% and 100% of median there is no room for a title, occupancy or make-ready surprise.
  • HOA and condominium plaintiffs. An association foreclosure sits behind the mortgage, not ahead of it.
  • Anything the title search has not cleared seven days before the sale. No search, no bid.
  • Commercial property, and anything above $1.5 million. Routed out of the program, not refused on merit — it is simply a different business.
