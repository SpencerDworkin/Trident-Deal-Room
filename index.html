Bought at the courthouse.

Trident acquires one-to-four-unit homes at New Jersey sheriff’s sales, where the opening bid is the lender’s judgment rather than the house’s value, and holds them under Housing Choice Voucher tenancy. Six counties. One written screen, run on every filing, every day.

At a sheriff’s sale the opening bid is the debt, not the house.

The judgment is what the borrower owes, not what the home is worth. The two can be compared in advance, from public records, for every scheduled sale, before anyone walks into the room. Trident does that every day, and buys only where they are furthest apart.

What we do

Screen. Buy. Hold.

Three jobs, in that order, done the same way for every property.

01 · Screen

Every filing, the same rules

Each scheduled sale in six counties is pulled daily, matched against a nine-rule buy box and scored on recorded debt against county median value. At the current pull the screen refuses 37% of the calendar on sight.

02 · Buy

For cash, to a written ceiling

Title, valuation, occupancy and liens are worked on a dated checklist before the sale. A principal attends, bids to a ceiling set from the underwriting, and stops there. Twenty percent at the gavel, the balance in thirty days.

03 · Hold

Voucher tenancy, for income

Each home is brought to the housing authority’s inspection standard and leased to a Housing Choice Voucher holder. The authority pays the larger part of the rent directly, every month. Trident does not flip.

The company

A Pursuit Lending Partners company

Trident is a subsidiary of Pursuit Lending Partners and is run by its two principals, Spencer Dworkin and Ayush Patel, who screen every filing, read every title, set every ceiling and stand at every sale themselves. There is no acquisitions team between the firm and the courthouse.

It is national in ambition and New Jersey in practice. Any judicial-foreclosure state with public sale calendars and a working voucher program is a candidate; the screen is pointed at a new county by loading its median value and its Fair Market Rent. New Jersey is where the book is.
