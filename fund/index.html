The fund

A private fund that buys at the courthouse and holds for income.

Trident is raising its first fund from family offices. The fund acquires one-to-four-unit homes at New Jersey sheriff’s sales, priced against recorded debt rather than market value, and holds them under Housing Choice Voucher tenancy. This page sets out how it is built, how capital moves through it, and what you receive as an investor. The confidential memorandum controls; this is the shape of it.

How capital moves

Commitments in. Properties bought for cash. Rents out.

Investors commit capital to the fund. The fund buys properties outright at sheriff’s sale — the auction requires cash, so there is no acquisition debt in the base structure — brings each one to the housing authority’s inspection standard, leases it to a voucher holder, and holds it. Rents pay operating costs and reserves first; what remains is distributed to investors on the terms in the memorandum.

The principals invest in the fund alongside you. The pace of deployment is set by the sheriff’s calendar and the screen, not by a quota: capital that has not found a property that clears the buy box stays in cash.

Why cash, not debt. A sheriff’s sale takes twenty percent at the gavel and the balance in thirty days, in certified funds. There is no financing contingency and no lender at the table. Buying for cash is not a choice the fund makes; it is the only way to buy at the courthouse. It also means no lender ranks ahead of investors on the properties.

Why cash sits undeployed. The screen refuses 37% of the current calendar and the checklist blocks bids on files that are not ready. Some quarters there will be more capital than qualifying sales. The fund holds it rather than bending the screen to spend it.

Reporting

You see the book, not a summary of it.

Trident runs on one working book: every filing in the covered counties, its screen result, and for every property the fund pursues, the file — title findings, lien reserve, occupancy, bid ceiling, what it paid, what it cost to make ready, when it leased and at what rent. Investor reporting is that book, quarterly, property by property.

There is nothing to reconcile between what we tell you and what we look at ourselves, because they are the same document. If a property disappoints, you will see it on the same page we do, in the same quarter.

Questions investors ask

The ones that come up on the first call.

Answered plainly here so the call can go further. Anything not covered, ask.

What am I actually invested in?

An interest in the fund, which owns the properties. Distributions come from the rents those properties generate after operating costs and reserves, and from any eventual sale. The fund is the owner of record; you own the fund.

Where does my money sit before it is deployed?

In cash, in the fund’s account. Properties are bought as they clear the screen and the pre-bid checklist, which means deployment follows the sheriff’s calendar rather than a schedule we set. We would rather hold cash than bid on a file the screen refused.

Are distributions guaranteed?

No. They are paid from the properties’ cash flow on the terms in the memorandum. If the properties do not produce it, it is not paid. Trident is newly formed; there is no track record behind the projections, only the method.

Is there debt on the properties?

Not in the base structure. A sheriff’s sale requires certified funds, so the fund buys for cash. If the fund were ever to borrow, the memorandum sets out the limits and you would be told.

What happens when a sale is adjourned, or we are outbid?

Nothing is spent. Capital for a property is committed only at the gavel. An adjourned sale moves on the calendar and the file waits; a sale where bidding passes our ceiling is walked away from. Most of the work on a file happens before the sale and costs time, not capital.

What are the main risks?

Surviving liens, occupancy and possession, condition unseen before purchase, the owner-occupant right of first refusal under New Jersey’s Community Wealth Preservation Program, adjournment, dependence on the voucher program, and the general risks of a newly formed manager. The strategy page sets out what the process does about each; the disclosures list them in full.

Who can invest, and what is the minimum?

Accredited investors and qualified purchasers, by private placement. The minimum commitment is $1,000,000. We are raising from family offices in New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Connecticut and Florida.

Can I see the properties?

Yes, after acquisition. Before the sale there is no interior access for anyone, including us. After the deed you are welcome to walk any property the fund owns. You will also see each one in the quarterly book, with its photographs.

How are you paid?

The manager’s compensation and participation are set out in the memorandum. The principals also invest in the fund on the same terms as other investors, and attend every sale themselves. We would rather explain the economics on a call than reduce them to a line here.
