Method

The buy box is written as rules. It runs on every filing, the same way, every time.

Trident has no track record to show you. What it has is a method that cannot be bent for a property we happen to like, and a current book that shows exactly how it sorts.

1

The buy box

A screen that lives in someone’s head drifts. This one is written down with numbers in it and run on every filing in every covered county in the same order. A principal cannot override a verdict; the only way to change it is to change the rule, for everyone, and say so.

A filing with no published judgment is included with a flag rather than refused; the judgment sits on the county detail page, not the sale list, and is pulled file by file before any bid.

3

What was refused, and why

A sheriff’s sale forgives nothing: senior liens, municipal charges and tax liens survive the deed. The refusals exist to keep us out of the files where the discount is an illusion.

  • Debt above the county median.The lender bids to its own debt; there is no discount to win.84
  • Thin.Between 0.8 and 1.0 there is no room for a title, occupancy or make-ready surprise.59
  • Association plaintiffs.A condominium or homeowners association foreclosing its own lien. Median ratio on these files is 0.08, which is the tell.20
  • Unit markers in the address.Likely condominium or multi-unit; outside the program.11
  • Below the $20,000 floor.A tax, association or utility lien rather than a first mortgage.1

5

The pre-bid checklist

Ten items on every file, each with a date that hangs off the sale. Nine block the bid. A file that reaches sale day with a blocking item open is not bid on.

  • T−21Order the sixty-year title search.Senior liens, municipal charges and federal tax liens survive the deed.
  • T−14Independent valuation.Assessor-derived values are estimates. Confirm the exit before a deposit is exposed.
  • T−10Search open code violations and municipal charges.Unpaid water and sewer, vacant-property registration and open violations ride with the parcel.
  • T−10Screen occupancy.Vacant, tenanted or owner-occupied. No interior access; good-cause eviction state. Price possession into make-ready now.
  • T−10Check owner-occupant first refusal.Under the Community Wealth Preservation Program a foreclosed owner-occupant may take the property at the opening bid.
  • T−7Read the title, set the lien reserve.What the deed extinguishes, separated from what survives. The surviving total goes into the basis.
  • T−5does not blockConfirm bidder registration and county sale terms.Deposit form, balance window and registration vary by county sheriff.
  • T−3Set the ceiling and the walk-away.From the underwriting, not the room. Never raised at the auction.
  • T−2Stage certified funds.Twenty percent of the winning bid, at the gavel.
  • T−1Confirm the sale has not been adjourned.Call plaintiff’s counsel. Adjournments are not re-advertised.
6

The four dates

All four are derived from the sale date, so when a sale is adjourned the set moves together. Twenty percent of the bid is committed at the gavel and rides until redemption lapses; missing the thirty-day balance forfeits it. The funds for the balance are in hand before the bid, not arranged after.

T − 7
Title search back. Lien reserve set; ceiling recomputed.
T
Sheriff’s sale. Twenty percent in certified funds at the gavel.
T + 10
Redemption lapses. The former owner’s right to redeem expires.
T + 30
Balance due. Eighty percent paid; sheriff’s deed delivered.
