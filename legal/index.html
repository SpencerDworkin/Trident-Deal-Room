Nothing on this website is a projection of, or a target for, any return.

Risk factors

The memorandum will contain a complete discussion of risks. Among those specific to this strategy:

  • Acquisition process. Properties are acquired through court supervised and other distressed sale processes in which the buyer has limited information, limited or no interior access, and limited recourse. Properties are bought as is.
  • Title and surviving encumbrances. A property may be conveyed subject to liens, charges and rights that the sale did not extinguish. Title searches may miss encumbrances, and an apparent discount may be reduced or eliminated by what survives.
  • Statutory rights of others. Owners, occupants and public bodies may hold statutory rights, including rights of redemption and rights of first refusal, that delay or defeat an acquisition.
  • Occupancy and possession. Properties may be occupied at purchase. Tenant protections in our markets are strong; obtaining possession can be lengthy and costly and may require payment to occupants.
  • Condition. Properties may have defects, environmental conditions or damage exceeding estimates, and may produce no income until stabilized.
  • Timing and deployment. Sales are frequently postponed or withdrawn. Capital may remain undeployed for extended periods. Deposits may be forfeited if a purchase is not completed.
  • Valuation. Values used in sourcing and underwriting are estimates and may be wrong.
  • Public housing program dependence. The strategy relies on tenancies supported by public housing programs that are federally funded and locally administered. Changes in funding, payment standards, inspection or administration could reduce rents, delay leasing or make properties ineligible.
  • Concentration. A limited number of properties in a limited number of markets.
  • Illiquidity. Interests are not transferable except as the governing documents permit. There is no market for them. Expect to hold for the full term.
  • Model and data risk. Sourcing depends on software and models that read public records. Those records may be incomplete, late or wrong, and a model may misread, misscore or omit an opportunity. Principal review may not catch every error.
  • Key persons. The strategy depends on two principals.
  • Regulatory and legal change. The sale processes, tenant protections and housing programs on which the strategy depends are subject to legislative, regulatory and judicial change.
  • Conflicts of interest. Trident and its principals will receive compensation from the fund as set out in the memorandum, will invest in the fund, may have interests in other ventures, and may face conflicts in allocating time and opportunities.
  • Leverage. The base structure uses no acquisition debt. If the fund later borrows within the limits the memorandum permits, lenders would rank ahead of investors on the properties concerned.
  • Fees and expenses. The fund will bear management fees, organizational and operating expenses, and the manager’s participation in profits, as set out in the memorandum. These reduce returns.
